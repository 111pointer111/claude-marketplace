Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart (1756-1791)

来源：Wikipedia (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Amadeus_Mozart)

---

Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart was born on 27 January 1756 at Getreidegasse 9 in Salzburg, then the capital of the Archbishopric of Salzburg within the Holy Roman Empire, to Leopold Mozart, a violinist and experienced pedagogue, and Anna Maria, née Pertl. Baptised the day after his birth at St. Rupert's Cathedral under the Latinised name *Joannes Chrysostomus Wolfgangus Theophilus Mozart*, he was the youngest of seven children, five of whom died in infancy. His elder sister was Maria Anna Mozart, known as "Nannerl."

Mozart displayed extraordinary precocity under his father's instruction. By age five he was competent on keyboard and violin, had begun composing, and performed before European royalty. Between 1762 and 1766, Leopold conducted a grand tour taking the family to Munich, Mannheim, Paris, London, The Hague, and Amsterdam. A second Italian journey from December 1769 to March 1771 saw Mozart admitted to the Accademia Filarmonica di Bologna, commissioned to write operas in Milan including *Mitridate, re di Ponto* (1770), and famously transcribe Gregorio Allegri's *Miserere* from memory. In 1770 he received the Order of the Golden Spur from Pope Clement XIV.

Following his final return from Italy on 13 March 1773, Mozart was employed as a court musician by Prince-Archbishop Hieronymus Colloredo in Salzburg. In January 1781 Mozart's opera *Idomeneo* premiered successfully in Munich. Summoned to Vienna in March 1781 by Archbishop Colloredo, he quarrelled with his employer and was dismissed in June 1781. Mozart settled in Vienna as a freelance performer and composer. On 4 August 1782 he married Constanze Weber.

In Vienna Mozart quickly established himself as the city's finest keyboard player, competing before Emperor Joseph II against Muzio Clementi on 24 December 1781. His opera *Die Entführung aus dem Serail* premiered triumphally on 16 July 1782. From 1782 to 1785 he composed more than a dozen piano concertos. On 14 December 1784 he was admitted to the Freemason lodge Zur Wohltätigkeit. His six string quartets dedicated to Joseph Haydn were composed between 1782 and 1785.

Collaborating with librettist Lorenzo Da Ponte, Mozart produced *Le nozze di Figaro* (Vienna, 1786), *Don Giovanni* (Prague, October 1787), and *Così fan tutte* (1790). Around 1788 his income declined by roughly 66% from his peak years. Despite this, he completed his last three symphonies—Nos. 39, 40, and 41 (the *Jupiter*)—during the summer of 1788.

His final productive year, 1791, brought *Die Zauberflöte*, the Piano Concerto No. 27 K. 595, the Clarinet Concerto K. 622, the String Quintet K. 614, the motet *Ave verum corpus* K. 618, and the unfinished Requiem K. 626. Mozart died at his Vienna home on 5 December 1791 at 12:55 a.m., aged 35. He was interred in a common grave at St. Marx Cemetery.
