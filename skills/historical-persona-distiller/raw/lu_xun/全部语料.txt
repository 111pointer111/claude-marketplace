# 一、生平传记

## 童年与绍兴 (1881-1897)

Lu Xun (魯迅), pen name of Zhou Shuren (周树人), was born Zhou Zhangshou on 25 September 1881 in Shaoxing, Zhejiang, into a declining family of landlords and scholar-officials. His paternal grandfather, Zhou Fuqing, had achieved the prestigious rank of Hanlin Academician. His father, Zhou Boyi, was stripped of his government position in 1893 for attempting to bribe an examination official, leading to the grandfather's imprisonment until 1901. The father subsequently fell into opium addiction and died of an asthma attack in 1896.

## 求学与弃医从文 (1898-1909)

After abandoning the imperial examination path in 1898, he attended the Jiangnan Naval Academy in Nanjing, then the School of Mines and Railways, graduating in 1902. He went to Japan in 1902, and enrolled at Sendai Medical School in 1904, where a pivotal lantern slide depicting the apathy of Chinese onlookers at a public execution during the Russo-Japanese War prompted him to abandon medicine for literature.

## 北京教育部与新文化运动 (1909-1917)

Lu Xun returned to China around 1909, holding teaching positions before securing employment at the Ministry of Education of the Beiyang government following the 1911 Xinhai Revolution. In 1917, he accepted Qian Xuantong's invitation to write for the journal La Jeunesse (New Youth).

## 文学创作高峰 (1918-1926)

In April 1918 published Diary of a Madman (狂人日记), the first modern Chinese novel written in vernacular language. He followed this with The True Story of Ah Q (阿Q正传, 1921), and collected his early stories in the volumes Nahan (Call to Arms, 1923) and Panghuang (Wandering, 1926).

## 上海杂文与左翼 (1927-1936)

He settled in Shanghai by late 1927, where he became co-founder of the League of Left-Wing Writers in 1930. He engaged increasingly with Marxist thought while maintaining an uneasy distance from the Chinese Communist Party. Lu Xun died of bronchial asthma on 19 October 1936 in Shanghai.
