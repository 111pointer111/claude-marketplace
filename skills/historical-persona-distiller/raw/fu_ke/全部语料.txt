PART 1 — BIOGRAPHY (Wikipedia)
================================================================

Paul-Michel Foucault (15 October 1926 – 25 June 1984) was a French historian of ideas and philosopher, also an author, literary critic, political activist, and teacher. His theories primarily addressed relationships between power, knowledge, and liberty, analyzing how they are used as social control through multiple institutions.

Life chronology:
- Born in Poitiers, France into upper-middle-class family
- Educated at Lycée Henri-IV, École Normale Supérieure, University of Paris
- Came under influence of Jean Hyppolite and Louis Althusser
- Joined French Communist Party in 1950; left in 1953
- Obtained DES in Philosophy 1949; agrégation in 1951
- Worked as psychology instructor at ENS (1951–55)
- Taught at Université de Lille (1953–54)
- Cultural diplomat at University of Uppsala, Sweden
- Worked at University of Warsaw, Poland (1958)
- Director of Institut français Hamburg (1958–1960)
- Published *Madness and Civilization* (1961)
- Taught at University of Clermont-Ferrand (1960–1966)
- Published *The Birth of the Clinic* (1963) and *The Order of Things* (1966)
- Lectured at University of Tunis (1966–1968)
- Head of philosophy at Paris VIII/Vincennes
- Elected to Collège de France (1970)
- Published *Discipline and Punish* (1975)
- Published *The History of Sexuality* Vol. 1 (1976)
- Reported on Iranian Revolution (1978)
- Died in Paris from HIV/AIDS complications

Notable ideas: biopower, disciplinary institution, discourse analysis, discursive formation, dispositif, épistémè, archaeology, genealogy, carceral archipelago, governmentality, heterotopia, gaze, power-knowledge, panopticism, subjectivation, parrhesia, epimeleia heautou, regimes of truth, Foucault's boomerang.

Influences: Hegel, Marx, Kant, Husserl, Heidegger, Nietzsche, Freud, Binswanger, Canguilhem, Althusser, Hyppolite.

PART 2 — LATER ASSESSMENT (SEP, head 2000 chars)
================================================================

French historian and philosopher born in Poitiers. Studied at the École Normale Supérieure (1946). Held positions at French universities; elected to the Collège de France (1969). Active politically; founded the Groupe d'information sur les prisons. Lectured frequently in the U.S., including at Berkeley. Died of AIDS in Paris.

Influenced by Merleau-Ponty, Heidegger, Hegel, and Marx (via Hyppolite and Althusser). Turned away from existentialism and Marxism. Canguilhem was a key supporter, providing a model of history of science. Engaged with structuralist linguistics (Saussure) and psychoanalysis (Lacan). Enthralled by Bataille and Blanchot's "limit-experiences."

Major works:
- *History of Madness* (1961): medical concept of "mental illness" masked social control of deviance from bourgeois norms.
- *The Birth of the Clinic* (1963): a more standard history of clinical medicine's emergence.
- *The Order of Things* (1966): "An Archaeology of the Human Sciences." Inverts Kant: asks what appears necessary might be contingent. Traces shifts from Renaissance resemblance to Classical representation to modern man/language.
- *The Archaeology of Knowledge* (1969): methodological treatise on "discursive formations" operating beneath subject consciousness.
- *Discipline and Punish* (1975): genealogy of the modern prison; introduces the Panopticon as a model of disciplinary power through surveillance, normalization, and examination. Power/knowledge are inseparable.
- *History of Sexuality, Vol. 1* (1976): rejects the "repressive hypothesis"; argues sexuality is *produced* by power through discourses (medical, psychological). Introduces biopower.
- *The Use of Pleasure* / *Care of the Self* (1984): studies ancient Greek/Roman ethics as an "aesthetics of existence."

Key concepts: archaeology, genealogy, power/discipline, biopolitics/biopower, governmentality, subjectivity, ancient ethics.

Posthumous materials: lectures at the Collège de France (1970–84) and *Les aveux de la chair* (2018) expanded his corpus, particularly on governmentality, neoliberalism, biopolitics, and the ancient idea of philosophy as a way of life.

Influence: major impact on feminist philosophy, queer theory (Butler's *Gender Trouble* builds on his work), political theory, sociology, and history of science.
