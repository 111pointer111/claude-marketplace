Leonardo da Vinci (1452-1519)

来源：Wikipedia (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_da_Vinci)

---

Leonardo da Vinci (15 April 1452 – 2 May 1519) was an Italian polymath of the High Renaissance, born Leonardo di ser Piero da Vinci, possibly in Anchiano near the Tuscan hill town of Vinci, about 35 kilometres from Florence. He was born out of wedlock to the Florentine notary Piero (Ser Piero) da Vinci and Caterina di Meo Lippi, a lower-class woman; both parents married others the year after his birth, and Leonardo eventually accumulated 16 much younger half-siblings, with whom he had little contact. Raised largely in the household of his paternal grandfather Antonio, he received only a basic informal education in writing, reading, and mathematics before his artistic talents redirected the family's attention.

Around 1464, at about age 14, he entered the workshop of the leading Florentine painter and sculptor Andrea del Verrocchio as a garzone, becoming a formal apprentice by 17 and remaining for seven years, where he was exposed to drafting, chemistry, metallurgy, mechanics, woodwork, and the fine arts alongside contemporaries such as Botticelli, Ghirlandaio, and Perugino. He collaborated with Verrocchio on The Baptism of Christ (c. 1472–1475), in the Uffizi, painting an angel whose skill reportedly surpassed his master's. By 1472, aged 20, he qualified as a master in the Guild of Saint Luke.

In 1482, sent by Lorenzo de' Medici, he entered the service of Ludovico Sforza, Duke of Milan. In Milan (c. 1482–1499), he painted the Virgin of the Rocks (c. 1483–1493) and The Last Supper (c. 1495–1498) for the monastery of Santa Maria delle Grazie — the most reproduced religious painting of all time — and devised plans for the colossal equestrian monument to Francesco Sforza known as the Gran Cavallo. He also produced the celebrated Vitruvian Man drawing (c. 1485, Accademia, Venice).

After Ludovico's overthrow by the French in 1500, Leonardo fled to Venice. He then served Cesare Borgia in 1502 as chief military engineer and architect. Returning to Florence, he rejoined the Guild of Saint Luke in October 1503 and began the portrait of Lisa del Giocondo — the Mona Lisa (c. 1503–1516/1519), the world's most famous individual painting.

In September 1513 he went to Rome under Pope Leo X. In 1516, at the invitation of Francis I, he entered French service and settled at the manor house Clos Lucé near the royal Château d'Amboise, supported by a pension of 10,000 scudi and frequently visited by the King. Leonardo died at Clos Lucé on 2 May 1519, aged 67. Vasari recounts that Francis held his head as he died. Per his will, sixty beggars with tapers followed his casket.
