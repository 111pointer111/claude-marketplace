Oscar Fingal O'Fflahertie Wills Wilde (1854-1900)

来源：Wikipedia (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscar_Wilde)

---

Oscar Fingal O'Fflahertie Wills Wilde was born on 16 October 1854 in Dublin, Ireland, and died on 30 November 1900 in Paris, France, at the Hôtel d'Alsace. He was buried at Père Lachaise Cemetery. He married Constance Lloyd on 29 May 1884, and they had two sons: Cyril (1885) and Vyvyan (1886).

Wilde was educated at Portora Royal School from 1864 to 1871, then read classics at Trinity College Dublin from 1871 to 1874 on a royal scholarship, where he won the Berkeley Gold Medal in Greek. He then read Greats at Magdalen College, Oxford, from 1874 to 1878, graduating with a double first and winning the 1878 Newdigate Prize for his poem "Ravenna."

He became a leading figure in the Aesthetic movement, influenced by Walter Pater and John Ruskin. His major works include The Happy Prince and Other Tales (1888), The Picture of Dorian Gray (1890), Intentions (1891), Salomé (written in French in 1891), Lady Windermere's Fan (1892), A Woman of No Importance (1893), An Ideal Husband (1895), The Importance of Being Earnest (1895), De Profundis (written in prison, published posthumously), and The Ballad of Reading Gaol (1898).

In 1886, Wilde met Robert Ross, and later Lord Alfred Douglas (Bosie), whose father was John Sholto Douglas, 9th Marquess of Queensberry. In 1895, Wilde sued Queensberry for criminal libel, but the case unraveled and led to Wilde's prosecution for gross indecency. He was convicted and sentenced to two years' hard labour, imprisoned from 1895 to 1897. He wrote De Profundis during his final year in prison. Upon release, he fled to France, never returning to Britain or Ireland.
