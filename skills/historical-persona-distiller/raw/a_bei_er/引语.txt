## On the quintic / impossibility theorem

1. "The mathematicians have been very much absorbed with finding the general solution of algebraic equations, and several of them have tried to prove the impossibility of it."
   — A Memoir on Algebraic Equations (1824), translated by W. H. Langdon

2. "It is impossible to solve the general equation of the fifth degree in terms of radicals."
   — A Memoir on Algebraic Equations (1824)

## On rigor in analysis (致 Hansteen 信, 1826)

3. "My work in the future must be devoted entirely to pure mathematics in its abstract meaning."
   — Letter to Christoffer Hansteen (1826)

4. 分析"lacks so completely all plan and system"，人们采用"this miserable way of concluding from the special to the general."
   — Letter to Christoffer Hansteen (1826), quoted by Øystein Ore and Morris Kline

## On divergent series (致 Holmboe 信, 1826)

5. "On the whole, Divergent series are the work of the Devil and it's a Shame that one dares base any Demonstration on them. You can get whatever result you want when you use them."
   — Letter to Bernt Michael Holmboe (January 1826), quoted by Arild Stubhaug

6. He cited the absurdity "0 = 1 − 2^n + 3^n − 4^n + etc."
   — Same source

## On French vs German mathematicians (致 Holmboe 信, ca. 1826)

7. "On the whole, I do not like the French as well as the Germans; the French are extremely reserved toward strangers… The most absolute egotism reigns everywhere."
   — Letter to Bernt Michael Holmboe (ca. 1826), quoted by Øystein Ore

## On studying masters (笔记本旁注, ca. 1826)

8. "It is readily seen that any theory written by Laplace will be superior to all produced of lower standing."
   — Marginal notebook note (ca. 1826), quoted by Øystein Ore

9. "It appears to me that if one wants to make progress in mathematics, one should study the masters and not the pupils."
   — Same source

## Other biographical notes (from Wikipedia)

10. Crelle informed Abel of his appointment as professor at the University of Berlin by letter — which arrived two days after Abel's death (6 April 1829).
11. Legendre called Abel's work on elliptic functions "a monument more lasting than bronze."
12. Hermite remarked: "Abel has left mathematicians enough to keep them busy for five hundred years."
